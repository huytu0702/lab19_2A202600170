# Nvidia

Source URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia

## Overview

Nvidia is a U.S. semiconductor and computing company founded in 1993, headquartered in Santa Clara, California. It began with graphics chips for gaming and evolved into a central supplier of hardware/software for AI, HPC, networking, and autonomous systems.

## Company history

After early product setbacks, Nvidia recovered with the RIVA and GeForce era, went public in 1999, and expanded through gaming and console partnerships. Over the 2000s and 2010s, it broadened into enterprise compute, professional graphics, and data-center systems. The Mellanox acquisition significantly strengthened networking and HPC capabilities.

## Core businesses

Nvidia’s portfolio includes GeForce gaming GPUs, RTX/pro visualization, data-center accelerators, DGX systems, automotive platforms, and software ecosystems such as CUDA, TensorRT, and Omniverse. As a fabless company, it relies on external manufacturing while focusing on design and platform integration.

## AI role

CUDA and GPU acceleration made Nvidia central to deep learning and large-scale model training. Its hardware became foundational for hyperscaler and enterprise AI deployments, with high-demand accelerator families driving major revenue and strategic influence.

## Acquisitions and partnerships

Nvidia expanded via acquisitions and partnerships across chips, networking, software, security, robotics, and AI infrastructure. The article references collaborations with cloud providers, enterprises, research organizations, and AI labs.

## Controversies

The article includes historical disputes (e.g., GTX 970 memory controversy, driver/open-source criticism, manufacturing-defect litigation) and newer regulatory/antitrust scrutiny tied to Nvidia’s AI market power and export-control dynamics.
