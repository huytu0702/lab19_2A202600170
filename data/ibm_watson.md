# IBM Watson

Source URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Watson

## Overview

IBM Watson is a question-answering computer system developed by IBM’s DeepQA project to process natural language and return evidence-ranked answers. It became famous for winning against top human champions on Jeopardy! in 2011.

## Development and Jeopardy era

Watson’s development focused on combining many NLP, retrieval, and reasoning techniques rather than one new algorithm. After several years of tuning, it reached competitive performance and won the public Jeopardy matches versus Ken Jennings and Brad Rutter. The event demonstrated strengths in speed, confidence-weighted wagering, and large-scale evidence scoring, while revealing contextual limitations.

## Technical architecture

Watson used IBM DeepQA and Apache UIMA across a distributed stack on POWER7 servers with large in-memory datasets. It combined hypothesis generation, evidence scoring, and ranking across many parallel methods, backed by encyclopedic and structured knowledge resources.

## Commercialization and healthcare

Following Jeopardy, IBM attempted broad commercialization across sectors (healthcare, retail, education, weather, support). Healthcare became a flagship area, with partnerships at major institutions and products such as Watson for Oncology.

## Outcomes and criticism

The article frames Watson as both a technical milestone and a commercialization cautionary tale: high performance on constrained QA tasks did not consistently translate into generalized enterprise outcomes. IBM later sold/spun off key Watson Health assets and shifted toward newer AI initiatives.
