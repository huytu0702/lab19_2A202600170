# OpenAI

Source URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI

## Overview

OpenAI is a U.S.-based artificial intelligence company headquartered in San Francisco. The article describes it as a research and product organization built around a for-profit public benefit corporation and a nonprofit foundation. OpenAI is best known for the GPT family of large language models, the DALL-E image models, and the Sora text-to-video line. Its release of ChatGPT in late 2022 is presented as a major turning point that helped drive broad public interest in generative AI.

The company began in 2015 as a nonprofit with a mission centered on ensuring that artificial general intelligence would serve humanity broadly. Over time, it moved toward commercial structures to support large-scale computing, hiring, and product development. The article emphasizes that OpenAI now operates with a mixed governance model that combines investor ownership, nonprofit oversight, and large corporate partnerships.

## History

OpenAI was founded in December 2015 by Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Ilya Sutskever, Greg Brockman, Trevor Blackwell, Vicki Cheung, Andrej Karpathy, Durk Kingma, John Schulman, Pamela Vagata, and Wojciech Zaremba. Early backers pledged substantial capital, but the article says actual funds received lagged behind those pledges. Musk said he was motivated in part by AI safety and long-term risk concerns.

In its early years, the company focused on open research, releasing OpenAI Gym in 2016 as a reinforcement-learning platform and Universe later that year as a way to evaluate general intelligence across games and web environments. Nvidia also donated its DGX-1 system to help train larger models.

In 2019, OpenAI shifted from a nonprofit-only model to a capped-profit structure. That change created a set of holding and operating entities, with the nonprofit retaining control and Microsoft obtaining a minority position in the for-profit side. The company said the new structure would make it easier to attract outside capital and compensate employees competitively.

The article then describes a major restructuring in 2025. OpenAI proposed converting the for-profit arm into a Delaware public benefit corporation and reducing the nonprofit’s direct control. By October 2025, the new structure had been adopted, with the nonprofit renamed the OpenAI Foundation and holding a 26% stake. Microsoft held 27%, while employees and other investors held the rest.

The company’s product history accelerated from 2019 onward. GPT-2 drew attention in 2019, GPT-3 followed in 2020, DALL-E arrived in 2021, and ChatGPT launched publicly in November 2022. GPT-4 was released in March 2023. In late 2023, OpenAI introduced GPTs for customized chatbots. In 2024 and 2025, the company expanded into reasoning systems, AI agents, and browser-based tools. The article also notes major internal turbulence, including Sam Altman’s temporary removal as CEO in November 2023 and his reinstatement days later after a board reset.

## Products and technology

OpenAI’s core technology stack is centered on foundation models and tools built around them. The article lists ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, Sora, Whisper, and an API for external developers among its main offerings. ChatGPT has grown into a broad consumer and enterprise product line with versions and add-ons including ChatGPT Plus, ChatGPT Pro, Deep Research, Search, Atlas, and business-oriented access.

The GPT series remains the company’s most important model family. GPT-2 became widely noticed for generating human-like text, while GPT-3 powered an early commercial API and a wide range of language tasks. GPT-4 expanded the system’s capabilities and was released to paying users and API customers. Later products included GPT-5.2, and the article says OpenAI shared early benchmark results for later models such as o3 and developed reasoning-focused systems such as o1.

OpenAI’s image and video systems are also prominent. DALL-E is described as a text-to-image model for generating complex pictures from prompts. Sora is the company’s text-to-video model. Whisper is a speech-recognition system used not only as a product but also internally for transcription work. The company’s product evolution suggests a shift from research prototypes toward a suite of consumer, developer, and enterprise offerings.

The article also highlights more agentic tools. Operator was released as a web-using AI agent for accomplishing user-defined tasks. Deep Research followed as another agent product, and the company later showcased Agent Builder, a drag-and-drop workflow tool for creating agent systems. ChatGPT Atlas, released in October 2025, integrated ChatGPT into a web browser.

OpenAI’s development philosophy has evolved. Early statements promoted openness and broad access, but the company later restricted some of its strongest models, citing safety and competitiveness. It also launched a superalignment effort in 2023 to study how future superintelligent systems might be aligned with human goals, though the project ended in 2024 after key leaders departed. The article says the company has increasingly emphasized safety controls while also facing criticism for reduced transparency.

## Partnerships/funding

Microsoft is the central strategic partner in the article. It first invested $1 billion in 2019, then added much larger commitments later. In January 2023, Microsoft announced another $10 billion investment over several years, with much of the spend tied to Azure usage. Microsoft also integrated OpenAI models into Bing, Edge, and Microsoft 365. After the 2025 restructuring, Microsoft’s stake was listed at 27%, and OpenAI agreed to purchase large amounts of Azure capacity.

OpenAI’s funding history is described as unusually large and fast-growing. The article states that the company raised $6.6 billion in late 2024, $40 billion in April 2025, and even larger amounts in 2026, reaching a reported $122 billion in committed capital by April 2026. Valuations climbed rapidly, with figures cited from $157 billion in 2024 to $852 billion in 2026.

The company also formed partnerships across the tech and media sectors. It worked with Apple to integrate ChatGPT features into Apple Intelligence, with Broadcom on custom AI chips, with Google Cloud for TPU access, with Oracle for large-scale computing, with AMD for multi-gigawatt chip purchases, and with NVIDIA on a major infrastructure memorandum. It acquired or partnered with multiple startups, including Global Illumination, Multi, io, Statsig, Roi, Software Applications Incorporated, Neptune, Torch, and Astral.

In government and defense, OpenAI supplied models to AI challenge programs and health-related agencies, signed a contract with the U.S. Department of Defense, and created an OpenAI for Government offering. The article says it worked with Anduril on drone defense systems and with the UK government on public-sector AI use. A proposed Stargate joint venture with Oracle, SoftBank, and MGX was framed as a massive AI infrastructure buildout.

## Controversies/legal issues

The article presents OpenAI as a frequent target of controversy and litigation. The biggest legal theme is copyright. Authors, publishers, newspapers, and media companies sued OpenAI and sometimes Microsoft, arguing that copyrighted text was used to train models without permission.

Privacy and data-handling issues are another major concern. The FTC investigated OpenAI over data security and privacy practices, including whether its systems generated harmful or false information. A separate incident involved an experimental sharing feature that made thousands of private ChatGPT conversations searchable through public search engines before it was removed.

Labor and governance issues appear repeatedly. Before 2024, departing employees reportedly had to sign long-term non-disparagement agreements, and OpenAI later released former workers from those terms. The 2023 removal of Sam Altman triggered a major internal crisis, leadership turnover, and employee threats to leave for Microsoft.

There are also military and surveillance concerns. OpenAI supplied tools to defense and intelligence-related programs, and the article says critics worried about possible use in surveillance or autonomous weapons.

The overall picture is of a company that expanded quickly into a dominant AI vendor while repeatedly drawing scrutiny over transparency, labor practices, governance, data use, and social impacts.
